## 2026-08-07 — SAST gate rename and iteration regularization (issue #11)

**Correlation-id:** `c29a5b1a-f2ce-4f60-ba24-c29fd289cc74`
**Iteration:** v0.15.0
**DS:** DS-63 (finish-quality-gates) · DS-62 (finish-substeps)
**Branch:** `refine/11-finish-v2` (derived from `master`)
**Issue:** #11
**Previous session:** `4a9e4c58` — [trail](2026-08-05-4a9e4c58.en.md)

### What changed?

Two corrections coming out of the lead developer's review on the Finish
refinement PR.

**1. The `sast` gate became `code-analysis`.** The name promised less than — and
something different from — what the gate delivers: the provisioned quality gate
evaluates violations, duplication **and** security hotspots — maintainability and
reliability, not security alone. SonarSource itself describes the product as a
*static analysis tool*; SAST is a subset of what it does. Three identifiers
renamed together:

| Before | After |
|---|---|
| `gates.sast` (manifest) | `gates.code-analysis` |
| `scripts/check-sast.sh` | `scripts/check-code-analysis.sh` |
| quality gate `prodops-sast` | `prodops-code-analysis` |

**2. v0.15.0 was opened.** The Finish refinement had been running without an
iteration — `iteration-plan.md` read "no active iteration". The work was
decomposed into DS-62, DS-63, and DS-64 under issue #11 and recorded as the
active iteration.

### Why?

**On the rename.** Calling the gate SAST implied that a red result is always a
security finding — when it may be a code smell or duplication. The ambiguity had
a practical cost: the repository uses "SCA" for `gates.dependencies` (Snyk) in
the literal sense of *Software Composition Analysis*, and adopting the acronym
for Sonar as well would make the same term mean two different things in
neighboring gates of the same manifest.

**On the iteration.** ProdOps infrastructure work does belong to an iteration in
this repository's pattern — v0.8.0, v0.11.0, and v0.12.0 are precedents, none of
them touching `api/src`. v0.8.0 states it in its objective: *"this iteration does
not deliver product — it delivers reliability and traceability of the delivery
framework itself"*. There was no reason for the Finish refinement to sit outside
the model.

### Boundary of the rename

"SAST" was **kept** where it describes CodeQL, which is SAST in the strict sense,
and in the explanation of why the gate no longer carries that name. The three
tools are now explicitly distinguished in the skills:

- `code-analysis` (Sonar, local) — analyzes the source code
- `dependencies` (Snyk, SCA) — analyzes third-party libraries
- CodeQL (SAST, remote in CI) — analyzes the source code for vulnerabilities

## 2026-08-09 — Rebase, trail reorganization, and materialization fix

**Correlation-id:** `c29a5b1a-f2ce-4f60-ba24-c29fd289cc74`
**Iteration:** v0.15.0
**Branch:** `refine/11-finish-v2` (rebased onto `master` @ `d9ddb28e`)
**Issue:** #11

### What changed?

**1. Rebase onto `master`.** `master` had moved 21 commits ahead of the branch
point. Zero file overlap between the two sides — the branch's 17 commits
replayed with no conflict.

**2. Orphan trails resolved.** This was the only open review comment. The 7
files were two distinct artifacts sharing one name: 3 real session trails
(session UUID, spec filename convention) and 4 trails scoped to an iteration or
card, with no session identity. The first 3 moved to `sessions/`; the other 4
were renamed to `iteration-trail*.md` and left in place.

**3. Root cause identified.** `templates/delivery/context-capsule.md` declared
`session-trail-dir: iterations/<version>/trails/`. Every capsule generated by
readiness pointed agents at the wrong directory — the trails did not escape by
carelessness, the template instructed it. Pinned to `trails/sessions/`.

**4. `materialize-skills.sh` fixed (3 defects).** Two prevented a complete
regeneration (`((COUNT++))` returning 1 under `set -e`; SIGPIPE from
`printf | head` under `pipefail`, surfacing only on the largest source). The
third was worse: the script assumed one skill = one file and never materialized
`steps/`, leaving dangling links in `finish`, `hack`, `sync` and `upstream` for
all three players — invisible because `--check` only inspected the top-level
`SKILL.md`.

**5. Session index rebuilt.** It listed 4 of 10 sessions and linked a file
deleted in `754873c5`.

### Why?

The Dev's instruction was "change freely to keep consistency", with the layer
rationale from `contributor-philosophy.md`. Applying that rationale led to the
**opposite** of the suggested change: moving `sessions/` into the iteration
folder would violate Rule 1 and the reproducible ideal — a session is addressed
by its ID, may span iterations, and its location must not depend on mutable
delivery state.

  Iteration Trail" section
- `trails/README.md` / `.en.md` — complete index
